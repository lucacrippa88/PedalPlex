The story behind PedalPlex

A 1972 Eko, too many pedals, and not enough time.

I've always loved music. I first picked up a guitar when I was 10 years old, and it wasn't just any guitar: it was my father's 1972 Made-in-Italy Eko Ranger 12-string. I was blown away by that full, rich sound. That's where I started playing acoustic guitar, and a few years later I bought my first amplifier and started exploring the world of electric guitar too.

And then came the pedals. I started buying a few, one after another, and began to realize just how huge the world of effects, amplifiers, and tone chasing really was.

But then a problem came up: space and time. With young kids at home, you don't have much of either, and playing guitar inevitably gets pushed a little further down the list. Let alone exploring new sounds: that ends up somewhere around third or fourth place.

So I asked myself: How can I quickly save my tones, with the little time I have available? And, even more importantly: How can I get the most out of my pedals without constantly having to buy new ones?

That's when I had the idea. And the idea was… taking pictures of my pedalboard. Just kidding.

Instead, I started using PowerPoint, recreating a digital version of my pedals and amplifier, turning the knobs and saving a tone on a new slide for every riff. I'll be honest: it didn't last long. While creating the very first slide, I was already thinking, "what are you doing?" By the end of the second one, I had already deleted everything.

The solution was to turn to my second passion: computer science. It had to be a small web app, running directly in the browser, where I could have digital versions of my pedals in a simple and fast way. And it worked. Pedalboards, effects, amplifiers, presets… I started saving all my tones with just a few clicks.

Then I realized the idea could scale. What had started as a small personal utility could become something more: an open-source project, freely available to everyone. From there, the catalog grew from my original 6 pedals to a few thousand. And I opened PedalPlex to everyone, with the idea of improving the experience of anyone who had the same problem I did. But not only them. Everyone else too, come on.

Because great tones deserve a save button. In general.
